# Undo is a stack of inverse commands, kept by the service

Remove and Delete act at once (⌫, ⌘⌫), so ⌘Z takes back the last of them (#150). What a Remove or a Delete changed is kept as what it takes to reverse it: the torrent's record (its id, destination, what's wanted, its seeding round), its .torrent and librqbit's `.bitv` (the pieces it had), hard-linked from the session folder while the torrent is still whole, with each of its files as it was then, and for a Delete each item it moved with where the Trash put it (`trashItemAtURL`'s resulting URL) and what it was there (volume and inode, and a file's length and modification time), recorded as each goes, in the write-ahead record a Delete under way already has. An undo is a command of its own: it puts back from the Trash what Delete moved, only what's there as it went, never over anything at the path, never through a symbolic link, and inside the destination only; then it adds the torrent again from what was kept, under its id. The pieces it had come back with it, without a full check, only while every file is as it was when it was kept (a rename to the Trash and back keeps it so); a file written or replaced since makes librqbit check them all, as librqbit itself only notices a file it can't read. One removed or deleted while Unchecked (its check cancelled, #88) comes back Unchecked, as it does after a restart: nothing checked its pieces. Like any command it can be refused with a typed reason the client words: the Trash was emptied, something is at the path, a folder on the way is a link, the drive isn't connected, the torrent was added again (the app says only "Already in the list": nothing was touched).

The stack lives in the service, kept in the data dir next to the store (`undo.json`, and a folder per kept torrent): one stack whatever made the change, so `phantom undo` works, and it outlasts the app and the service restarting. It's 20 deep; the oldest goes. Remove, Delete and Move to Trash (below) are on it; Pause, Resume, a priority, Add, Check and the settings aren't, and there's no redo. When each action and each undo runs, and which action a ⌘Z takes, is one pure machine, `lifecycle::undo`, which the GUI's fake runs too: see *Steps and lanes* below.

**A refused undo is dropped, not kept.** Most reasons never fix themselves (an emptied Trash, a torrent added again, a file put where one goes back), and one that stayed on top would hide every older entry behind it, so ⌘Z would keep saying the same thing. Telling the reasons that may pass (a drive away) from the rest would be a rule per reason. Nothing is lost but the shortcut: the notice says why, and the files are still in the Trash, where Finder's Put Back works. An undo that can't finish once it's begun (a move back failing, or adding the torrent again failing once its files are back) returns what it moved to the Trash first, so every undo does all or nothing, and a refused one has changed nothing. What can be told before anything moves is (every item in the Trash as it went, each path free, the folders on the way real ones, the destination there and named, the .torrent kept readable), so the rest is rare; what then can't go back to the Trash is named in the refusal.

Everything is written ahead: what brings a torrent back is in `undo.json` before its record leaves `engine.json`, and an undo before anything moves. An undo under way when Phantom stops is finished first; one cut short by a crash is finished at the next start, and a partly deleted torrent still in the list is taken out then. An item no longer in the Trash is one it put back, if it's at its path as it went; otherwise it was emptied from the Trash meanwhile and, while its path is free, stays gone, and its files download again (#162). An item that then can't go back (something is at its path, an emptied one's too, or a folder on the way is a link) ends the undo as any undo that can't finish ends: what it put back goes to the Trash again, including what went back before the crash, and the undo is refused. A Delete whose torrent the crash left in the list with all its files gone is taken off at the start, its entry written again in its own place. So is a Remove the crash cut short between those two saves, with the entry and the stash it kept then (#162). An add of a torrent something runs on (its Delete, an undo bringing it back) waits for it: it's then in the list, or the add is a new one. `undo.json` never keeps the service from starting: what can't be read (a newer build's entry, a broken file) is dropped, entry by entry, and the file kept beside it as `undo.invalid.json`.

In the app, Remove and Delete each tell what they did in the status bar ("Deleted Album"), its popover with an Undo of that action, on while it's the newest on the stack. ⌘Z and Edit ▸ Undo (macOS's standard item, which hands the key to the window) undo the last, whatever made it.

**An undone action's notice leaves (#200).** The service says why each entry left the stack, where it acts: `Snapshot::left` lists the last 64 entries that left, each with a `Leave` (kept in `undo.json` with the stack, so a restart doesn't lose it, and repeated in every snapshot until newer ones push it out; a client takes each by its seq once). *Undone*: its undo finished (a Move to Trash whose Trash was partly emptied is undone as any is). *Refused*: its undo was, whoever asked (the CLI's too), with the typed reason. *Aged*: pushed past the last 20. *Absorbed* `by` a newer Delete of the torrent that took its place. *Dropped*: it changed nothing, or a stop dropped it while it waited. The record is made at the end of an undo, not when ⌘Z takes the entry. The app never works out why from the stack's changes (snapshots are the latest only, so a push and an undo between two of them would pass for something else).

**Undo is optimistic in the notices.** The moment an Undo is pressed (⌘Z, Edit ▸ Undo or a notice's own button) the notices of that action leave the list, its partial-failure notice with them: a notice is out while an undo is under way for every action it tells of. If the undo is refused (this app's own answer, or the service's `Leave::Refused`, whoever asked) they come back in their place, the Undo off with "Couldn't be undone: <why>". When it finishes (`Leave::Undone`) they're gone for good, and "X is back" is told once the files are back. A notice whose entry was *absorbed* follows the newer Delete (a partly failed Delete deleted again): it stays, and goes when that is undone; its Undo says a newer step comes first. A notice of several actions stays while part of it isn't under way. Aged and dropped entries change no notice. Each reason an Undo is off has its own words: refused, "Couldn't be undone: <why>"; past the last 20, "Only the last 20 Removes, Deletes and Moves to Trash can be undone"; a newer step on top or a newer Delete, "A newer Remove, Delete or Move to Trash comes first…"; part undone, "Part of it was undone already" (until a selection's Remove or Delete is one undo step, #235).

## Move to Trash is on the stack too (#151)

⌘⌫ on selected files sets them Off, then moves those on disk to the Trash (that order, so a size check never finds a file the torrent gets gone), on the trashing a Delete uses. It's one undo step per ⌘⌫: an entry whose torrent stays in the list (`Back::Files`), with the items it moved (as a Delete's) and each file asked about with the priority it had. An Undo puts back from the Trash what's still there, and each file's priority as it was, in the same all-or-nothing way (a failure puts back what had come back); the rules of the stack stand: it lives in the service, a refused undo is dropped, one that has nothing to put back (every item gone from the Trash) is refused as any is.

**A partly emptied Trash restores what's left.** Unlike a Delete's, which brings back a torrent whole or not at all, a Move to Trash is made of independent files: one emptied from the Trash doesn't make the others unrecoverable. Each file still in the Trash goes back, and gets its previous priority (only those: something at the path of one emptied from the Trash, a stub a shared piece made, say, isn't asked about and isn't touched, since the undo adds nothing there); one that was moved and isn't there stays Off (Normal would make it Missing files), and the rest of the files asked about get theirs.

**Cut short by a crash**, an undo of a Move to Trash is finished at the next start by the same rule as any (#162): what's still in the Trash goes back (an item emptied from it meanwhile stays gone while its path is free; one the user put back by hand in Finder can't be told from one the undo put back itself), and priorities are restored; with something of the user's where an item goes back, nothing is put back, what had gone back before the crash returns to the Trash, and the undo is refused and dropped. Commands for its torrent wait for it as they do for a running one.

Its torrent must be in the list, the same one (added again since is another), and one that moving files to the Trash would do something to now (not deleting, not partly deleted, destination reachable): the lifecycle's own answer, in the words of Undo. It takes the place of no entry and none takes its: the torrent never left, so it's a step of its own. Each item it moves is written ahead into its step, as a Delete's are into the torrent's record (#190): a crash ends it with what it moved, which Undo puts back. The undo sets the priorities Off first and Normal last, so one that fails halfway never leaves a file Normal that it then moves back to the Trash.

## Steps and lanes (#190)

Every Remove, Delete and Move to Trash is a transaction. Each torrent's work runs in order, and different torrents' work runs side by side.

- **A step** is an action, the undo of one, or any other command for a torrent. It goes into the **lane** of every torrent it touches, all at once, as it comes, so lanes that share steps always agree on their order. It runs once it's first in all of them. One machine holds the lanes and the stack, as the lifecycle is one: it says which steps to start, and the actor only moves files and reports back (`Moved`, written ahead; `Finished`; `Failed`).
- **The stack's order is the order of the presses and commands.** An action is on the stack from the moment its command comes, running. It ends committed with what undoes it (failing partway, with what did happen), or leaves the stack if it changed nothing. A Delete with nothing left to move (every file already moved by Moves to Trash) still takes its torrent off the list, so it's committed, and its undo brings the torrent back as a Remove's does; before #190 it left nothing to undo. A refused command never gets a step: one decided at once is refused before anything shows, and one decided later leaves the stack as it's refused. Which entry a ⌘Z takes never depends on timing; only the disk work overlaps, and only across torrents.
- **⌘Z never waits.** It takes the top step at once and leaves no turn to wait for. On one done, its undo goes into the lanes of the torrents it touched, as a step of its own, so it runs after what came before it there. On one still running, the item in flight ends, everything moved so far is rolled back in the action's place on its lanes, and the step leaves. On one that hasn't started, the step is dropped. Several ⌘Z each take the next step down. *Delete A, Delete B, ⌘Z, ⌘Z*: the two put-backs run side by side. *Trash file 1 of A, trash file 2 of A, Delete A, ⌘Z ×3*: one after another on A's lane, as file 2 goes back into A's folder only once the Delete's undo has brought the folder back. A command an Undo takes back is answered `Undone`; the app tells nothing of it, and the Undo tells what came back.
- **What clients see is what the snapshot lists:** the torrents as their steps make them, with no in-between words.
  - From the moment a Remove or a Delete comes, its torrent isn't listed, and a command for it is refused as for one not in the list. A Delete that fails brings the torrent back, with its error.
  - Once ⌘Z takes it back, the torrent is listed again at once, in its old place and status, with no details until its files are back; it starts once they are. The undo is over once its files are back and librqbit has the torrent: from then on the row shows what librqbit does with it (Checking, if it checks it in full), and what waited behind the undo runs in its turn. A put-back that fails takes a torrent that had left the list away again, with the refusal; the rollback of an aborted Delete that can't finish leaves its torrent listed, partly deleted. Either way the commands that waited behind it are answered `Undone`, so the app shows one notice, the undo's, and the row shows the torrent as it really is.
  - The files of a Move to Trash, or of its undo, show their new priorities at once.
  - A command that waits its turn behind a step shows done at once (a Pause shows Paused; a priority its new value, for a torrent in the list; a Check Checking, with nothing checked yet), and a new one is judged on the torrent as clients see it, as the steps before it will leave it: back, once ⌘Z took its Delete back; paused, once a Pause before it has run. If it fails once its turn comes, the list follows, and its client hears why. A Cancel check that finds no check at its turn (the check it was asked of ended meanwhile) is done: no check runs, as asked. A torrent an undo is bringing back shows its status and actions this way at once; its Size and priorities follow once it's back, as its details do.
  - The state a torrent shows while an undo brings it back is rebuilt from the undo's own record (the one it will restore), not kept from when the action came, so it's the same after a restart or a crash.
  - There's no **Deleting** status and no "Undoing…".
- **The lifecycle's Deleting phase stays, internal only.** It still stops the torrent in librqbit, keeps its storage untouched, and remembers the phase to go back to. It's never listed (`status` is none). Nothing reaches it either: a command for a torrent whose Delete ⌘Z took back is judged on what clients see (back as it was) and waits on the lane behind the rollback; the lifecycle meets it once the torrent is back.
- **Phantom's storage leaves a file moved from its path as it went**, so a Move to Trash never waits for librqbit (#217's fourth and fifth reviews). librqbit keeps a torrent's files open, and as a check ends (one paused by Cancel check too) it sets each file's length through them, which moves a file's modification time even at the same length. A file moved to the Trash meanwhile would no longer be the one that went, and its undo would find nothing in the Trash. Every storage librqbit gets is Phantom's own, from a first add on, and a Move to Trash holds the torrent's files still around each item it moves.
  - A file in the Trash for an undo is read-only to librqbit (#218): it isn't changed where it went (its length isn't set, nothing is written into it), and it's read there, through the file librqbit has open or, after a restart, opened where the Trash put it. A check, a start and an upload find it as before the move: a peer that asks for one of its pieces gets it (a failed read would disconnect it, as librqbit does with any peer whose piece it can't read). If the Trash can't be opened (macOS's privacy rules might keep the service out of the user's Trash), its reads fail as for a file not there, and its undo checks the torrent, which has less of it than is on disk.
  - Which files are in the Trash for an undo is what the undo stack says: each running Move to Trash's, each entry's still in the Trash as it went, each undo's while it runs. A file is one from the moment it moves, before the stack says it. It's never made again at its path, even for a piece it shares with a file the torrent gets: what librqbit writes into it is kept aside, in the data dir (`undo-parts/`, by torrent, file and offset), and read over the file, so the piece checks and the other file completes, across restarts too. The undo writes it into the file before it ends. It goes when its entry leaves the stack, or the Trash no longer has the file; a file no undo will put back any more is still read where it went, and made again at its path when librqbit writes into it, as any file set Off that isn't there.
  - The undo leaves the torrent as it was before the move: it renames the files back and sets the priorities. The move records what librqbit had of each file once they're Off (in the undo log, so a restart or a crash keeps it; nothing during a check). If librqbit has more of a file put back now (a shared piece came meanwhile) and what was kept of it couldn't go into the file, or it couldn't open the file where it went, the undo checks the torrent. One Unchecked stays Unchecked.
  - Whether a file is still where it was opened is looked at on disk only for the files Moves to Trash took. A file moved by hand is noticed by the size check, as Missing files; the check that brings it back makes it again at its path.
  - The storage doesn't follow a rename: one by hand of the torrent's own folder while it downloads has the next write make the folder again. Moving a torrent's files (#24) must add it to librqbit again, or keep it untouched across the move.
- **#179's waiting folds into the lanes.** A Move to Trash's torrent's other commands, and those of a torrent whose files an undo puts back, wait as any command waits on its torrent's lane. Nothing waits on another torrent.
- **A stop** lets what runs end. A shutdown drops what waits (each client hears the service is stopping), but the undos, which were pressed: they still run. An engine restart for new settings (the listening port, UPnP) drops nothing: the service carries on, so everything waiting runs first, as asked (#198's S4). With a long Delete or put-back under way, the restart waits for all of it.
- **A crash** is no event of a machine that dies with the process. At the next start, what waited goes but the undos, and what ran is carried out again, by #181's rules, from what each wrote ahead. The undos the crash cut short are told apart first: a rollback the start makes, of an action aborted before the crash, starts afresh, checked and taken out as any undo. A Delete is finished as what's left of it; a Move to Trash ends with what it moved; an undo, or the rollback of an aborted action, resumes. A Remove cut short goes, or, past its entry, is finished (#162).
- Each command arrives on its own today (a Delete of several torrents is one command per torrent), so every step touches one torrent; the machine takes a set all the same.

## Considered options

- **An event log replayed backwards.** librqbit and the disk can't be replayed or rewound: the files move, the Trash renames them, librqbit forgets the pieces with the torrent. An entry that knows how to reverse its own action is what can be carried out, and checked against the disk as it is now.
- **Commands that change the stack wait behind a Delete under way** (#198). A Remove, a Delete or an Undo of any torrent waited for another torrent's Delete, and for an undo under way, then ran in the order it came. It kept the stack in order, but a Delete on a stalled drive held up every other torrent's Remove and every ⌘Z, possibly for good, with nothing on screen to say why: it needed in-between words ("Removing…", "Undoing…") to explain the wait. Every new kind of action had to join the rule, and a replayed Undo that ran in the background let what came after it run first, breaking the order again. Putting each action on the stack as it comes makes the order structural, and lanes keep each wait to the torrent it's about. #198 had rejected reserving a slot on the ground that an Undo would wait on a slot not filled yet, a Delete that moves nothing leaves a gap, and the slot must outlast a crash. Here, a ⌘Z on a running step aborts it, a step that changes nothing leaves the stack, and steps are written ahead.
- **The stack in the GUI.** Each client would keep its own, lost when the app quits, and the CLI's actions couldn't be undone from the app or the other way round. The service already owns the torrents and the Trash's records.
- **Keeping a refused undo.** See above.
- **Finishing an undo cut short with what's back** (#162): adding the torrent again with the items put back before the crash, and the ones blocked since showing as Missing files. A blocked item is blocked because of what's at its path, or a link on the way, and adding the torrent again would have librqbit write its file over that, or through the link. Making that safe would need a torrent kept in the list that never touches some of its own paths. An undo after a crash does what any other undo does instead. Only an item emptied from the Trash, whose path is free, is left out.
- **Adding the torrent again from its .torrent alone.** librqbit then checks every piece. Keeping its `.bitv` brings it back with its progress; librqbit still reads a piece of each file it says it has, so a file that's gone makes it check them all.
