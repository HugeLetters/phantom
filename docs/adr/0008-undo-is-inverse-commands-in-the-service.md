# Undo is a stack of inverse commands, kept by the service

Remove and Delete act at once (⌫, ⌘⌫), so ⌘Z takes back the last of them (#150). What a Remove or a Delete changed is kept as what it takes to reverse it: the torrent's record (its id, destination, what's wanted, its seeding round), its .torrent and librqbit's `.bitv` (the pieces it had), hard-linked from the session folder while the torrent is still whole, with each of its files as it was then, and for a Delete each item it moved with where the Trash put it (`trashItemAtURL`'s resulting URL) and what it was there (volume and inode, and a file's length and modification time), recorded as each goes, in the write-ahead record a Delete under way already has. An undo is a command of its own: it puts back from the Trash what Delete moved, only what's there as it went, never over anything at the path, never through a symbolic link, and inside the destination only; then it adds the torrent again from what was kept, under its id. The pieces it had come back with it, without a full check, only while every file is as it was when it was kept (a rename to the Trash and back keeps it so); a file written or replaced since makes librqbit check them all, as librqbit itself only notices a file it can't read. One removed or deleted while Unchecked (its check cancelled, #88) comes back Unchecked, as it does after a restart: nothing checked its pieces. Like any command it can be refused with a typed reason the client words: the Trash was emptied, something is at the path, a folder on the way is a link, the drive isn't connected, the torrent was added again (the app says only "Already in the list": nothing was touched).

The stack lives in the service, kept in the data dir next to the store (`undo.json`, and a folder per kept torrent): one stack whatever made the change, so `phantom undo` works, and it outlasts the app and the service restarting. It's 20 deep; the oldest goes. Remove, Delete and Move to Trash (below) are on it; Pause, Resume, a priority, Add, Check and the settings aren't, and there's no redo. When each action and each undo runs, and which action a ⌘Z takes, is one pure machine, `lifecycle::undo`, which the GUI's fake runs too: see *Steps and lanes* below.

**A refused undo is dropped, not kept.** Most reasons never fix themselves (an emptied Trash, a torrent added again, a file put where one goes back), and one that stayed on top would hide every older entry behind it, so ⌘Z would keep saying the same thing. Telling the reasons that may pass (a drive away) from the rest would be a rule per reason. Nothing is lost but the shortcut: the notice says why, and the files are still in the Trash, where Finder's Put Back works. An undo that can't finish once it's begun (a move back failing, or adding the torrent again failing once its files are back) returns what it moved to the Trash first, so every undo does all or nothing, and a refused one has changed nothing. What can be told before anything moves is (every item in the Trash as it went, each path free, the folders on the way real ones, the destination there and named, the .torrent kept readable), so the rest is rare; what then can't go back to the Trash is named in the refusal.

Everything is written ahead: what brings a torrent back is in `undo.json` before its record leaves the database (`records.db`, [ADR 0012](0012-the-record-in-a-database.md)), and an undo before anything moves. An undo under way when Phantom stops is finished first; one cut short by a crash is finished at the next start, and a partly deleted torrent still in the list is taken out then. An item no longer in the Trash is one it put back, if it's at its path as it went; otherwise it was emptied from the Trash meanwhile and, while its path is free, stays gone, and its files download again (#162). An item that then can't go back (something is at its path, an emptied one's too, or a folder on the way is a link) ends the undo as any undo that can't finish ends: what it put back goes to the Trash again, including what went back before the crash, and the undo is refused. A Delete whose torrent the crash left in the list with all its files gone is taken off at the start, its entry written again in its own place. So is a Remove the crash cut short between those two saves, with the entry and the stash it kept then (#162). An add of a torrent something runs on (its Delete, an undo bringing it back) waits for it: it's then in the list, or the add is a new one. `undo.json` never keeps the service from starting: what can't be read (a newer build's entry, a broken file) is dropped, entry by entry, and the file kept beside it as `undo.invalid.json`.

In the app, Remove and Delete each tell what they did in the status bar ("Deleted Album"), its popover with an Undo of that action, on while it's the newest on the stack. ⌘Z and Edit ▸ Undo (macOS's standard item, which hands the key to the window) undo the newest change, an action or a setting's, whatever made it: see *One order* below.

**An undone action's notice leaves (#200).** The service says why each entry left the stack, where it acts: `Snapshot::left` lists the last 64 entries that left, each with a `Leave` (kept in `undo.json` with the stack, so a restart doesn't lose it, and repeated in every snapshot until newer ones push it out; a client takes each by its seq once). *Undone*: its undo finished (a Move to Trash whose Trash was partly emptied is undone as any is); of a selection, with each of its torrents that didn't come back and why (#235), which every client tells, whoever asked. *Refused*: its undo was, whoever asked (the CLI's too), with the typed reason. *Aged*: pushed past the last 20. *Absorbed* `by` a newer Delete of the torrent that took its place. *Moved*: a Move to Trash whose torrent's files moved to another destination since (#24). *Dropped*: it changed nothing, or a stop dropped it while it waited. The record is made at the end of an undo, not when ⌘Z takes the entry. The app never works out why from the stack's changes (snapshots are the latest only, so a push and an undo between two of them would pass for something else).

**Undo is optimistic in the notices.** The moment an Undo is pressed (⌘Z, Edit ▸ Undo or a notice's own button, or another client's: its entry is off the stack with no word yet why) the notices of that action leave the list, its partial-failure notice with them: a notice is out while an undo is under way for every action it tells of. If the undo is refused (this app's own answer, or the service's `Leave::Refused`, whoever asked) they come back in their place, the Undo off with "Couldn't be undone: <why>". When it finishes (`Leave::Undone`) they're gone for good, and "Back in the list" (the name in its list) is told once the files are back. A notice whose entry was *absorbed* follows the newer Delete (a partly failed Delete deleted again): it stays, and goes when that is undone; its Undo says a newer step comes first. Aged and dropped entries change no notice. Each reason an Undo is off has its own words: refused, "Couldn't be undone: <why>"; past the last 20, "Only the last 20 Removes, Deletes and Moves to Trash can be undone"; a newer step on top or a newer Delete, "A newer Remove, Delete or Move to Trash comes first…"; its torrent moved since, "Moved to another folder since".

## Move to Trash is on the stack too (#151)

⌘⌫ on selected files sets those on disk Off, then moves them to the Trash (one not on disk is left as it is, and with none there nothing is on the stack: #276) (that order, so a size check never finds a file the torrent gets gone), on the trashing a Delete uses. It's one undo step per ⌘⌫: an entry whose torrent stays in the list (`Back::Files`), with the items it moved (as a Delete's) and each file asked about with the priority it had. An Undo puts back from the Trash what's still there, and each file's priority as it was, in the same all-or-nothing way (a failure puts back what had come back); the rules of the stack stand: it lives in the service, a refused undo is dropped, one that has nothing to put back (every item gone from the Trash) is refused as any is.

**A partly emptied Trash restores what's left.** Unlike a Delete's, which brings back a torrent whole or not at all, a Move to Trash is made of independent files: one emptied from the Trash doesn't make the others unrecoverable. Each file still in the Trash goes back, and gets its previous priority (only those: something at the path of one emptied from the Trash, a stub a shared piece made, say, isn't asked about and isn't touched, since the undo adds nothing there); one that was moved and isn't there stays Off (Normal would make it Missing files), and the rest of the files asked about get theirs.

**Set Normal again, it's downloaded again, and the move stays undoable (#276).** A file in the Trash for an undo shows none of it, though librqbit reads it where it went (so an undo needs no check): what it would download, set Normal. Set Normal, librqbit's pieces of it are let go of, it's made at its path as an add makes a file a torrent gets (librqbit, adding the torrent back, opens it there), and downloaded from nothing; the torrent isn't Missing files, which is for a file it gets that vanished. Its Move to Trash can still be undone: ⌘Z sends that new copy to the Trash (never erased; given back if the undo can't finish), puts the old one back by a rename, and checks the torrent, as librqbit's pieces of it are the new copy's. The old copy always wins, whatever the sizes; ⌘Z never waits. Something at the path that isn't the torrent's new copy (a file of the user's at a path the torrent doesn't get) still refuses the undo.

**Cut short by a crash**, an undo of a Move to Trash is finished at the next start by the same rule as any (#162): what's still in the Trash goes back (an item emptied from it meanwhile stays gone while its path is free; one the user put back by hand in Finder can't be told from one the undo put back itself), and priorities are restored; with something of the user's where an item goes back, nothing is put back, what had gone back before the crash returns to the Trash, and the undo is refused and dropped. Commands for its torrent wait for it as they do for a running one.

Its torrent must be in the list, the same one (added again since is another), and one that moving files to the Trash would do something to now (not deleting, not partly deleted, destination reachable): the lifecycle's own answer, in the words of Undo. It takes the place of no entry and none takes its: the torrent never left, so it's a step of its own. Each item it moves is written ahead into its step, as a Delete's are into the torrent's record (#190): a crash ends it with what it moved, which Undo puts back. The undo sets the priorities Off first and Normal last, so one that fails halfway never leaves a file Normal that it then moves back to the Trash.

## A Rename is on the stack too (#331)

A Rename is an action whose part runs a Move within its torrent's destination under the new name (`Back::Renamed`, the name it had): its undo renames it back the same way, never over something at the old name (refused, as any undo, and dropped). The torrent never leaves the list, so it absorbs nothing and nothing absorbs it. It isn't a Move into another folder, so it drops no Move to Trash of its torrent: those are under it on the stack, and come back once it's undone; one left after a refused rename back finds its paths changed and is refused as moved. ⌘Z on a Rename whose torrent is moving to another folder, or has a Move waiting on its lane, refuses it at once (`Command::Refuse`, left refused for `Busy`) rather than wait behind the Move: every other undo waits its turn on the lane, but a Move can take minutes.

## Steps and lanes (#190)

Every Remove, Delete and Move to Trash is a transaction. Each torrent's work runs in order, and different torrents' work runs side by side.

- **A step** is an action, the undo of one, or any other command for a torrent. It goes into the **lane** of every torrent it touches, all at once, as it comes, so lanes that share steps always agree on their order. It runs once it's first in all of them.
- **One press is one step (#235).** A Remove or a Delete of a selection is one command with all its ids (`Request::Torrents`, `phantom remove a b c` too), so one step over their lanes, one entry on the stack, and one ⌘Z brings all of it back. The step has a **part** per torrent, which runs and reports on its own (`Moved`, `Finished`, `Failed`, by seq and torrent); the step is over once every part is. One that failed in part (2 removed, 1 refused) is one entry holding what did happen; the command's answer says which failed and why. Its undo has a part per torrent it changed, each all or nothing on its own: the client hears what came back and, of each that didn't, why; the entry left the stack *undone* if any came back, with each part that didn't and why, so every client tells which (`phantom undo` exits 1, as a Remove failed in part does), else *refused* for the first part's reason. A newer Delete of one of its torrents takes that part's place (absorbs it), and the entry leaves only once no part is left; one ⌘Z took while it ran does too, as its rollback puts back the older one's items with its own. Any other command of several (Pause, Resume) is a step per torrent, as it's on no stack. One machine holds the lanes and the stack, as the lifecycle is one: it says which steps to start, and the actor only moves files and reports back (`Moved`, written ahead; `Finished`; `Failed`).
- **The stack's order is the order of the presses and commands.** An action is on the stack from the moment its command comes, running. It ends committed with what undoes it (failing partway, with what did happen), or leaves the stack if it changed nothing. A Delete with nothing left to move (every file already moved by Moves to Trash) still takes its torrent off the list, so it's committed, and its undo brings the torrent back as a Remove's does; before #190 it left nothing to undo. A refused command never gets a step: one decided at once is refused before anything shows, and one decided later leaves the stack as it's refused. Which entry a ⌘Z takes never depends on timing; only the disk work overlaps, and only across torrents.
- **⌘Z never waits.** It takes the top step at once and leaves no turn to wait for. On one done, its undo goes into the lanes of the torrents it touched, as a step of its own, so it runs after what came before it there. On one still running, the item in flight ends, everything moved so far is rolled back in the action's place on its lanes, and the step leaves. On one that hasn't started, the step is dropped. Several ⌘Z each take the next step down. *Delete A, Delete B, ⌘Z, ⌘Z*: the two put-backs run side by side. *Trash file 1 of A, trash file 2 of A, Delete A, ⌘Z ×3*: one after another on A's lane, as file 2 goes back into A's folder only once the Delete's undo has brought the folder back. A command an Undo takes back is answered `Undone`; the app tells nothing of it, and the Undo tells what came back.
- **What clients see is what the snapshot lists:** the torrents as their steps make them, with no in-between words.
  - From the moment a Remove or a Delete comes, its torrent isn't listed, and a command for it is refused as for one not in the list. A Delete that fails brings the torrent back, with its error.
  - Once ⌘Z takes it back, the torrent is listed again at once, in its old place and status, with no details until its files are back; it starts once they are. The undo is over once its files are back and librqbit has the torrent: from then on the row shows what librqbit does with it (Checking, if it checks it in full), and what waited behind the undo runs in its turn. A put-back that fails takes a torrent that had left the list away again, with the refusal; the rollback of an aborted Delete that can't finish leaves its torrent listed, partly deleted. Either way the commands that waited behind it are answered `Undone`, so the app shows one notice, the undo's, and the row shows the torrent as it really is.
  - The files of a Move to Trash, or of its undo, show their new priorities at once.
  - A command that waits its turn behind a step shows done at once (a Pause shows Paused; a priority its new value, for a torrent in the list; a Check Checking, with nothing checked yet), and a new one is judged on the torrent as clients see it, as the steps before it will leave it: back, once ⌘Z took its Delete back; paused, once a Pause before it has run. If it fails once its turn comes, the list follows, and its client hears why. A Cancel check that finds no check at its turn (the check it was asked of ended meanwhile) is done: no check runs, as asked. A torrent an undo is bringing back shows its status and actions this way at once; its Size and priorities follow once it's back, as its details do.
  - The state a torrent shows while an undo brings it back is rebuilt from the undo's own record (the one it will restore), not kept from when the action came, so it's the same after a restart or a crash.
  - There's no **Deleting** status and no "Undoing…".
- **The lifecycle's Deleting phase stays, internal only.** It still stops the torrent in librqbit, keeps its storage untouched, and remembers the phase to go back to. It's never listed (`status` is none). Nothing reaches it either: a command for a torrent whose Delete ⌘Z took back is judged on what clients see (back as it was) and waits on the lane behind the rollback; the lifecycle meets it once the torrent is back.
- **Phantom's storage leaves a file moved from its path as it went**, so a Move to Trash never waits for librqbit (#217's fourth and fifth reviews). librqbit keeps a torrent's files open, and as a check ends (one paused by Cancel check too) it sets each file's length through them, which moves a file's modification time even at the same length. A file moved to the Trash meanwhile would no longer be the one that went, and its undo would find nothing in the Trash. Every storage librqbit gets is Phantom's own, from a first add on, and a Move to Trash holds the torrent's files still around each item it moves.
  - A file in the Trash for an undo is read-only to librqbit (#218): it isn't changed where it went (its length isn't set, nothing is written into it), and it's read there, through the file librqbit has open or, after a restart, opened where the Trash put it. A check, a start and an upload find it as before the move: a peer that asks for one of its pieces gets it (a failed read would disconnect it, as librqbit does with any peer whose piece it can't read). If the Trash can't be opened (macOS's privacy rules might keep the service out of the user's Trash), its reads fail as for a file not there, and its undo checks the torrent, which has less of it than is on disk.
  - Which files are in the Trash for an undo is what the undo stack says: each running Move to Trash's, each entry's still in the Trash as it went, each undo's while it runs. A file is one from the moment it moves, before the stack says it. It's never made again at its path, even for a piece it shares with a file the torrent gets: what librqbit writes into it is kept aside, in the data dir (`undo-parts/`, by torrent, file and offset), and read over the file, so the piece checks and the other file completes, across restarts too. The undo writes it into the file before it ends. It goes when its entry leaves the stack, or the Trash no longer has the file; a file no undo will put back any more is still read where it went, and made again at its path when librqbit writes into it, as any file set Off that isn't there.
  - The undo leaves the torrent as it was before the move: it renames the files back and sets the priorities. The move records what librqbit had of each file once they're Off (in the undo log, so a restart or a crash keeps it; nothing during a check). If librqbit has more of a file put back now (a shared piece came meanwhile) and what was kept of it couldn't go into the file, or it couldn't open the file where it went, the undo checks the torrent. So it does for a file the move let go of because it was replaced by hand before it went (#268): librqbit had none of the file that went, and the check reads it where it's back, all of it if it was written back as it was (#328). One Unchecked stays Unchecked.
  - **Once it's gone from the Trash** (emptied, or deleted from it by hand) while librqbit has it open, Phantom lets go of it (#234), so its space is freed at once and Finder doesn't find it in use. librqbit can't stop offering a piece it has (it has no way to take one off what it has, and the protocol none to tell a peer), and it drops a peer that asks for one it can't read, or for one it doesn't have. So the files Moves to Trash took are looked at every size check (every few seconds), off the actor in a thread per torrent with a timeout, as the disks are (an `fstat` of an open file can hang on a network volume): one whose open file has no name left anywhere is written ahead in the torrent's record and closed, which never waits for librqbit (one it's reading is closed at a later look). Then its torrent is kept aside (its .torrent and `.bitv`, as for Undo), taken out of librqbit and added back from there, stopped, with that file's pieces cleared from the `.bitv`, those it shares with a neighbour too, and no full check: librqbit samples a few of the pieces it has, as at a start. Its peers drop once and connect again, and none of those pieces is offered any more. Its row keeps its status throughout: its lifecycle hears nothing until librqbit is done sampling, then it runs as wanted. A piece it shared with a neighbour still in the Trash is read from what's kept of that one; one shared with a file the torrent gets downloads again, which makes the emptied file again at its path as a file set Off that isn't there. Only a torrent librqbit runs or holds stopped as it is is taken out, with nothing on its lane; one checked, out of reach, in error or deleted, partly or not, waits, its file closed already; a check finds the pieces gone, and once librqbit has none of them the let-go ends there, with no take-out. Each step is written ahead in the torrent's record: a crash before librqbit let go of the torrent has its pieces cleared where librqbit saved them, before the engine adds it; one after has them put back from what was kept, without them, then it's added as any torrent is (#45): out of librqbit while its destination isn't connected (Destination unavailable, checked once it's back). If that can't be done, it's tried again at each look, and an add that fails is an error that keeps its record and what was kept, for Resume or Check to try again. Removed or deleted meanwhile, it's as any: its let-go is finished first, a Delete moves its files, found from its record, and either undo brings it back with its pieces. Either way the start finishes it or keeps it. Its undo is as for any file emptied from the Trash: the file stays gone, and Off.
  - Whether a file is still where it was opened is looked at on disk only for the files Moves to Trash took, and for those a torrent is missing. A file moved by hand is noticed by the size check, as Missing files; the check that brings it back makes it again at its path. **One deleted by hand** (#45, decided on #244) has librqbit's handle closed at once, so its space is freed: the look the size checks make (the one above, off the actor) closes each file its torrent is missing that has no name left anywhere, waiting for nothing (one librqbit is reading is closed at a later look). The torrent stays in librqbit, stopped, Missing files; Check or Resume brings it back, checked, as before. A file moved elsewhere keeps its space, and stays open. Set Off while it's missing (#244's re-check), and unreadable (not in the Trash for an undo, nor just put back by one), it's let go of as one gone from the Trash is: written ahead in the record as it's set Off, closed by the look if it isn't yet, and the torrent, which doesn't start meanwhile, is added back without its pieces once it's free. librqbit's bitfield is every piece it has, a file Off or not, so it would otherwise offer pieces it can't read, and drop each peer that asks. **One set Off first, then deleted or moved away by hand** (to Finder's Trash, emptied or not: #276) is let go of the same way: the size check, which looks at every file's path, finds an Off file librqbit has some of with nothing at its path, not in the Trash for an undo, while its torrent is Ready or Missing files with nothing on its lane (a Delete, a Move to Trash or a move takes files away on purpose), writes it ahead, and closes it. It shows none of it from then, and while librqbit samples what it's added back with, its row shows that, not what librqbit had (#273). A read of no bytes, which librqbit makes of the file a piece starts right after, always succeeds, so a file closed or not there never drops a peer that asked for its neighbour.
  - The storage doesn't follow a rename: one by hand of the torrent's own folder while it downloads has the next write make the folder again. Moving a torrent's files (#24) adds it to librqbit again.
  - **Nor a symbolic link at a file's path** (#267): one there, dangling or not, is never the torrent's file. Every file is opened `O_NOFOLLOW`, so none is made, written, resized or read through it, and the size check finds the file missing, as with something other than a file there: at an add the file is left unopened, and a write into it fails. Taking the link away (or putting the file back) and checking makes the file at its path, as for any file missing. A link to a folder on the way is followed: where the person keeps the torrent's files, or its destination, is their choice, and a dangling one makes nothing (a folder isn't made through a link either).
- **Moves run one at a time, in the order asked** (#270). Every Move, of any torrent, waits for each older one: a Move joins its torrent's lane only at its turn, once every older Move has ended (`lifecycle::undo`'s `promote`), so a selection's Moves, one step each in the list's order, run one after another, which suits copies across drives. Till its turn it's on no lane, so it holds none: what comes for its torrent meanwhile (a Pause, a priority, a Move to Trash) runs at once, as nothing waits on another torrent, and the Move, behind whatever runs on that lane when its turn comes, is judged on the torrent as it is then (a destination gone meanwhile refuses it). Clients see it as Waiting to move (`lifecycle::undo::view`), its rates and peers as they are, as it runs meanwhile; it's not a phase of its lifecycle: librqbit goes on telling of the torrent (a check ends, a download finishes), which a phase wrapping what it was would have to follow. Cancel move, a Remove or a Delete takes it out as it comes, on no lane (it would wait for what it cancels); the Move's client hears it was cancelled. A clash is answered for the files it was told of, as they were (volume and inode, length, modification time: `Request::Clashes` finds them, the answer sends them back): at the Move's turn anything else at its paths, or one of them changed, refuses it (`ClashChanged`) and the queue goes on, so nothing goes to the Trash, or is taken as the torrent's, that the person wasn't asked about (a file put there while it waited, an earlier Move's into the same paths). That can't see a file an earlier Move took over with Use existing, unchanged, so a file another torrent in the list has (at its paths, whether or not a file is there now, or where its Move takes it from, stages or puts it; the same file on disk however it's reached) refuses the Move first, whatever the answer (`AnothersFiles`): one torrent's Move never replaces nor takes another's files, nor a seeding torrent's in that folder. `Clashes` names that torrent (`Clashing::other`), for the dialog to offer a new name instead; an earlier torrent of the same list that would move to the same paths counts as one (the second is asked about, not refused after the queue), and where another torrent's folder is gone its paths are compared as `name_free` compares names (Unicode composed, case folded), from the nearest folder on disk. Rename…'s field opens with a name `Request::FreeName` finds free at that moment. It tries ` (2)` to ` (999)` (the stem cut to fit a name's 255 bytes), ends at any refusal that isn't a name being taken, and with no name to give the field opens with the torrent's own. A Move under a new name (`TorrentCommand::Move::name`, the clash dialog's Rename…) runs as #331's Rename runs within a destination: anything at the new name at its turn refuses it (`NameTaken`), as another torrent's place there does (`NameOfAnother`), and the record's name on disk changes with its destination in one written-ahead save. What Use existing keeps is checked again once the rest has landed, before the Move is written ahead as landed: swapped or written since, the Move goes back. That narrows the window between the check and librqbit opening the file by path, but can't close it: a file swapped in that window is taken as the torrent's, and the torrent's own copy goes to the Trash, where it can be got back. A stop drops what waits, so quitting puts back the Move under way and no other starts. An engine restart for new settings waits only for the Move under way: no other joins its lane from the moment it begins (one at its turn but not started leaves it, so what's behind it runs), Cancel move meanwhile goes to the engine stopping, and those still waiting, with their clients, go to the engine that starts next, which runs them in their order. So a batch's request holds no handle on the engine while it waits for its answer (`Engine::send_moves`), and the restart, which waits for every handle, isn't held by it. A crash drops them, as what waits.
- **A Move is a step on its torrent's lane, on no stack** (#24). It runs until its files are moved or back where they were: what comes for the torrent meanwhile waits behind it, shown done at once (Pause, Resume, a priority), but Check and another Move, refused while it moves. A Remove or a Delete cancels a move whose files aren't in place yet, as it comes (one still waiting its turn too), then runs once the torrent is back where it was. A move a crash cut short is finished, or put back, at the next start, on no step: what comes for its torrent meanwhile is held, as while librqbit samples its pieces. Undo doesn't take a move back. A Move goes ahead while files of the torrent are in the Trash for an undo: as it starts, each Move to Trash of the torrent leaves the stack, *moved* (its undo would put the files back in the old destination), and nothing else does; the files stay in the Trash, where Finder's Put Back works. Its notice's Undo turns off: "Moved to another folder since". The rule is the stack's (`lifecycle::undo`, `Observed::Moving`), the engine's and the fake's. A move found something unexpected at a new path (the person's) always goes back, even with a file of it deleted by hand meanwhile: that file stays deleted, what was copied of it goes to the Trash, and the torrent is back where it was, Missing files.
- **#179's waiting folds into the lanes.** A Move to Trash's torrent's other commands, and those of a torrent whose files an undo puts back, wait as any command waits on its torrent's lane. Nothing waits on another torrent.
- **librqbit's sampling runs on the lane** (#45, decided on #244). A torrent added back with the pieces librqbit saved (at every start, by an undo, by a let-go) is listed at once as its record says, and nothing shows while librqbit reads a few of those pieces to trust them. A step that comes meanwhile is held, as behind a step running on its lane: a command shows done at once, as one waiting its turn does, and runs right after; what comes after it waits behind it. ⌘Z on a held action drops it as one that waited its turn: nothing moved, to the Trash or back. One whose destination goes meanwhile is taken out of librqbit, which ends the sampling, and what's held runs. A full check, if librqbit does one, is a check like any, shown and cancellable. A stop starts what's held, without waiting for the sampling.
- **A stop** lets what runs end. A shutdown drops what waits (each client hears the service is stopping), but the undos, which were pressed: they still run. A Move whose files aren't in place yet is put back at a shutdown rather than waited for: quitting stays quick, and the move can be asked again (#24). An engine restart for new settings (the listening port, UPnP) drops nothing: the service carries on, so everything waiting runs first, as asked (#198's S4), but a Move waiting its turn, which goes on in the engine that starts next (#270). With a long Delete, put-back or Move under way, the restart waits for all of it.
- **A crash** is no event of a machine that dies with the process. At the next start, what waited goes but the undos, and what ran is carried out again, by #181's rules, from what each wrote ahead. The undos the crash cut short are told apart first: a rollback the start makes, of an action aborted before the crash, starts afresh, checked and taken out as any undo. A Delete is finished as what's left of it; a Move to Trash ends with what it moved; an undo, or the rollback of an aborted action, resumes. A Remove cut short goes, or, past its entry, is finished (#162).

## Settings changes (#221)

⌘Z takes back the newest change of a setting, whoever made it, when it's the newest change of all (*One order*, below), and Edit ▸ Undo names it; `phantom settings undo` the newest of a service setting. They're not on the torrents' stack. A step is a change that takes effect: Return in a field, a switch, a folder, an App icon, a theme setting. The last 20, in memory only, no redo.

Settings live in two places, so there are two keepers, and neither holds the other's. **The service** keeps each change of its own settings (`settings.json`), from every client, with what the setting was before; `UndoSetting` takes its newest back, as a change by hand would be applied, but a default folder that can't be reached now goes back all the same (its row says why). The change is taken off first, so a refused undo is gone. **The app** keeps what each change of its own settings (`gui.json`: the App icon, the Active Look Preset) was before; the service never stores them.

**The app keeps the order.** It has one list of the changes it knows, in the order it learned of them: its own as they're made (a service setting's the moment it's sent, `by` the app's id for it), another client's as the service's snapshots list them. The service names the change it made both in its answer to the change and in its snapshots (with the app's id), so the app keeps its own change in its place, and a ⌘Z already on it, whichever reaches it first: the connections a command and the stream take promise no order between them. Settings go by their own count (`Configured::at`): a snapshot's that are older than those the app has, or than a change an answer named, change nothing. ⌘Z takes the newest. An app setting's it undoes itself; a service setting's it asks the service for, which always undoes its own newest and says which: so the service's settings go back in the order the service made the changes, and one another client made between the press and the answer is the one undone. A change the service no longer lists (undone by another client, pushed out, gone with a service that stopped) leaves the list. Every snapshot carries the settings and the changes, numbered on their own (`Configured::at`), so Settings, open, follows another client at once.

## One order (#255)

⌘Z anywhere, in the main window or in Settings, takes the newest change: a Remove, Delete or Move to Trash, or a settings change, from any client. Edit ▸ Undo names whichever is next ("Undo Remove of Album", "Undo Port Change"). The CLI keeps its two commands: `phantom undo` for the torrents' stack, `phantom settings undo` for the service's settings.

- **The torrent actions join the app's one list** as the app learns of them, as the settings changes do. The app's own when it sends them: pending until the stack lists its entry (the first after the entries the app knew then, of one of its torrents) or the command's answer names it, keeping its place and a ⌘Z already on it; so ⌫ then ⌘Z takes the Remove, however soon. Another client's from the undo stack each snapshot lists (`Snapshot::undo`), newer ones on top. The stack goes by the snapshots' `(epoch, version)`, taken from every snapshot whatever it was built for: an older snapshot changes nothing. An entry the stack no longer lists (undone by another client, refused, pushed past the last 20, absorbed by a newer Delete) leaves the list. There's no clock: the list's order is when the app learned of each change.
- **One snapshot, two kinds:** of another client's settings change and torrent action that one snapshot lists, the settings change counts as the older. The snapshot doesn't say which came first; ordering them truly would need one count for both kinds, the stamps rejected below.
- **Entries are told apart by their seq alone**, and none is hidden for being numbered below one listed before: a service that lost its `undo.json` numbers them from 1 again, and its actions are the app's to undo all the same.
- **A torrent action's undo the app asks of the service**, which undoes the top of its stack and says which entry it took, undone or refused (`Undone::undo`, `Refusal::Undo`, with its torrents): so the actions go back in the order of the stack, as the service's settings go back in the order the service made them. The entry taken leaves the list for good, even if a snapshot from before the answer still lists it (a refusal comes with no snapshot after it), and so does the app's own action still pending that made it; the one asked for, if the service took another (another client's, made since), can be taken again. An answer that names none (nothing to undo, the connection lost) leaves the one asked for to be taken again: the stack outlasts the service. Likewise a settings undo's answer names the app's id for the change it undid (`SettingChange::by`): a change the app sent that the service undid, before its own answer came, leaves at once.
- **Each kind keeps its own depth**: the app's last 20 settings changes, and every entry of the torrents' stack (20). Settings changes never push a torrent action out of the list, so ⌘Z reaches every action the stack holds.
- **Where the undo shows**: a setting's opens Settings at its tab (if it was closed), its control focused once the settings are read, with its notice ("Port is back to 6881"); a refused one opens nothing and says why. An action's brings its torrents back in the list, behind Settings if it's open, with its notice ("Back in the list", the torrent's name in its list).
- **A notice's own Undo** takes its action while it's the top of the stack, as before (#200), whatever settings change is newer: it's on that action, so it never takes a setting. It shows ⌘Z beside it only while ⌘Z would take that same action.

## Considered options

- **Two orders: ⌘Z in the main window for the torrent actions, ⌘Z in Settings for the settings** (#221). It kept a ⌘Z in the list from changing a setting where nothing showed it. Once a setting's undo opens Settings at it, focused, with its notice, nothing changes unseen, and two orders meant ⌘Z skipped the newest change depending on where the focus was.

- **Stamps, compared in the service** (#221 as first designed): each app change stamped with the number and run of the service's newest change, and ⌘Z asking the service to undo its own if newer than the stamp. Correct for another client's changes, but a stamp is the app's view of the service when the app change is made, so a change the app itself sent and the service hasn't listed yet (a new port, while the torrents restart) counts as newer than an App icon picked after it: ⌘Z would take the port first. The list puts the app's own changes in the order they were made, needs no numbers compared and no run, and a service that restarts simply lists nothing of before.
- **The app's settings kept by the service**, so one stack holds both: the service would store what the app owns, and the app couldn't change its look without it.

- **An event log replayed backwards.** librqbit and the disk can't be replayed or rewound: the files move, the Trash renames them, librqbit forgets the pieces with the torrent. An entry that knows how to reverse its own action is what can be carried out, and checked against the disk as it is now.
- **Commands that change the stack wait behind a Delete under way** (#198). A Remove, a Delete or an Undo of any torrent waited for another torrent's Delete, and for an undo under way, then ran in the order it came. It kept the stack in order, but a Delete on a stalled drive held up every other torrent's Remove and every ⌘Z, possibly for good, with nothing on screen to say why: it needed in-between words ("Removing…", "Undoing…") to explain the wait. Every new kind of action had to join the rule, and a replayed Undo that ran in the background let what came after it run first, breaking the order again. Putting each action on the stack as it comes makes the order structural, and lanes keep each wait to the torrent it's about. #198 had rejected reserving a slot on the ground that an Undo would wait on a slot not filled yet, a Delete that moves nothing leaves a gap, and the slot must outlast a crash. Here, a ⌘Z on a running step aborts it, a step that changes nothing leaves the stack, and steps are written ahead.
- **The stack in the GUI.** Each client would keep its own, lost when the app quits, and the CLI's actions couldn't be undone from the app or the other way round. The service already owns the torrents and the Trash's records.
- **Keeping a refused undo.** See above.
- **Finishing an undo cut short with what's back** (#162): adding the torrent again with the items put back before the crash, and the ones blocked since showing as Missing files. A blocked item is blocked because of what's at its path, or a link on the way, and adding the torrent again would have librqbit write its file over that, or through the link. Making that safe would need a torrent kept in the list that never touches some of its own paths. An undo after a crash does what any other undo does instead. Only an item emptied from the Trash, whose path is free, is left out.
- **Adding the torrent again from its .torrent alone.** librqbit then checks every piece. Keeping its `.bitv` brings it back with its progress; librqbit still reads a piece of each file it says it has, so a file that's gone makes it check them all.
